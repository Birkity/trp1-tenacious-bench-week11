# Audit Memo: Why Tenacious-Bench Is Needed

tau2-Bench retail measures whether an agent completes transactional workflows under tool constraints. It does not measure whether a B2B sales agent should have sent a message in the first place, whether the pitch matches a company's buying window, or whether a claim is grounded in public signal. The Week 10 `trace_log.jsonl` confirms this mismatch: those records are retail-domain simulations with rewards, costs, and task IDs that say almost nothing about Tenacious-specific sales quality. A retail success can coexist with a sales failure.

The Tenacious failure mode is not grammar or task completion but semantic fit. The style guide requires grounded, honest, non-condescending outreach, and Week 10 showed that surface checks can pass while the substance is wrong.

The clearest evidence comes from contrasting SnapTrade, WiseiTech, and the clean growth traces. In `artifacts/snaptrade/email_log.jsonl`, the brief marks SnapTrade `icp_segment=ambiguous` with `hiring_velocity.delta_pct=-60.0`. The email still pitches "bottlenecks integrating new APIs" and offers to "augment your team." Tone warnings were empty. WiseiTech is sharper: `delta_pct=-100.0`, `signal_strength=weak`, `icp_segment=ambiguous`, yet the email offers to "augment existing Python, Big Data, and ML capabilities." Both traces prove the system confuses stack match with ICP fit: a company with Python in its job descriptions is not evidence it is in market for staff augmentation. Compare with Arcana Analytics (`delta_pct=100.0`, Series A, `icp_segment=Segment 1`), PulseSight (`delta_pct=133.33`, Series A), and StreamlineOps (`delta_pct=120.0`, Series B, urgent Kafka/Spark demand): these are credible pitches because velocity, funding, segment, and stack all align.

The probe library adds evidence of risky edges that generic benchmarks miss. Probes 8-10 target ICP misclassification: layoffs, fully staffed teams, and NestJS stack questions must override the original segment assignment. Probes 11-13 target signal over-claiming: when a prospect challenges "doubling," stale funding, or a role-type mismatch, the system must acknowledge the limit rather than defend the original claim. Probes 18-20 show tone drift around self-disclosed AI gaps; a warm lead can be lost if the system becomes condescending. Probes 24-26 cover data provenance and weak-signal honesty. Probe 30 combines a positive hiring signal, a NestJS bench trap, and pricing in one reply, proving that real sales conversations do not isolate dimensions.

The core benchmark problems are ICP-pitch alignment and signal directionality, with grounding fidelity as the base requirement. If a brief says "Ambiguous," the email must ask a qualifying question, not make a product claim. If hiring velocity is materially negative, growth frames such as scaling, bottlenecks, or augmentation must not appear. Numeric, date, funding, and stack claims must trace to the brief, bench summary, or prior thread.

Tenacious-Bench must measure five dimensions. D1 Grounding Fidelity: every claim must be supported by supplied evidence. D2 ICP-Pitch Alignment: pitch frame must match Segment 1, 2, 3, or 4, or end with a qualifying question for Ambiguous. D3 Signal Directionality: growth language on negative hiring velocity is a hard fail. D4 Tone Compliance: style-guide violations become hard failures. D5 Format Compliance: subject length, body length, no cold booking links. Together these dimensions make the hidden Week 10 failure machine-verifiable: a stranger can score whether a short, clean email is actually safe to send.
